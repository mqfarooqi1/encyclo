Built the other way round

Most reference apps are written first and sourced afterwards, if at all. This one starts from the evidence and will not publish an article without it.

📖

Four reading levels, properly written

Ages 6–8, 9–12, teen and adult — each written for its audience, not chopped down from the adult text. The software rejects a children's level that is a copy of a harder one.

🔍

Every source states its claim

A citation does not just point at a museum. It records the specific thing that museum is being asked to support, and the reader can see it.

⚖️

Uncertainty stays visible

Facts, estimates, interpretations and genuinely debated points are marked differently. T. rex's weight is an estimate, and it says so.

🧭

Explorer Trails for children

Quizzing as a journey: stations unlock in turn along a trail, each awarding a badge. No streaks, no daily targets, nothing designed to pull a child back tomorrow.

🕸️

Connections you can explore

A knowledge graph links articles by real relationships, a timeline runs from 4.5 billion years ago to today, and comparison mode puts two subjects side by side.

🔌

Genuinely offline

No CDN, no web fonts, no analytics, no outbound requests at all. Unplug the network and everything still works, because there was never anything to fetch.

The rules it holds itself to

These are enforced by the software, not just intended. Content that breaks them fails to load.

No claim without evidence. An article published with no citations is a hard error, not a warning.
History cannot be rewritten. Revisions are append-only. An edit creates a new version; nothing is overwritten.
Your notes are yours. Bookmarks and progress live in a separate file that content updates never touch, and never leave the device.
Nothing is invented. The optional assistant may only restate what the articles and their sources already say — and refuses to answer when the encyclopaedia does not cover the question.
Ranking is not popularity. Search weights exact matches, source quality and completeness. View counts are not a ranking signal.
An old article is not a wrong one. Review dates reflect how fast a subject moves. Population figures need yearly review; proved mathematics does not expire.

Download and run

One file. Open it. There is no installer, no build step and nothing to configure.

1

Get the app for your machine

A single self-contained file. Python is not required.

↓ Windows ↓ macOS ↓ Linux

Not code-signed: Windows SmartScreen says the publisher is unknown, so choose More info → Run anyway. On macOS, right-click and choose Open. On macOS and Linux, chmod +x the file first.

2

Open it

The first launch takes about twenty seconds while it builds the 13,703-article database and search index on your machine, showing progress as it goes. Then your browser opens at http://127.0.0.1:8000. Every launch after that is immediate.

3

Or run it from source

Needs Python 3.11 or newer, and nothing else.

python desktop.py

git clone https://github.com/mqfarooqi1/encyclo.git

Where your things are kept: bookmarks, notes and reading progress live in a separate file from the library, so replacing the app never touches them. Nothing is sent anywhere — there is nowhere to send it to.

What it does not do yet

Stated here rather than discovered later.

  • Only 57 articles are written here. The other 13,646 are lead sections imported under CC BY-SA 4.0. They are labelled as imported on every page, cite the exact revision they came from, and are kept out of Kids Mode because nobody has read them. Writing an article at four levels with per-claim citations is the slow part, and that is the work that remains.
  • No photographs. The licensing model is built and complete, but shipping images means verifying every licence first. Article covers are generated instead, so nothing unlicensed is distributed.
  • Source links show "unverified". Correct and deliberate. A link is only promoted once a checker has confirmed it, which needs network access you have to switch on.
  • Maps show places, not a base map. Location data and spatial indexing are in place; an offline map layer needs a licensed tile set.
  • It is a local web app, not a packaged desktop app. It runs in your browser from your own machine. A desktop wrapper can be added without changing the engine.
